# Inspiration — generosity, and the right person to guide this work

> **Our reading of public teaching. Not an endorsement.** Pastor Jentezen
> Franklin, senior pastor of Free Chapel, has no involvement in `philanthropisch`
> and has not been asked about it. Nothing here should be presented as his view
> of this project. Everything below is paraphrased from the three public pages
> listed under *Sources*, read on 2026-09-12.

This file does two jobs. It records ideas about generosity worth building on,
and it uses them to describe **the kind of person** who fits the guide role in
[PILOTS.md](needs/food-insecurity/PILOTS.md).

## 1. What the teaching says, in brief

| Theme | Our paraphrase | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Generosity isn't only money** | People give time, treasure, and talent. The greatest givers often have the least: the pensioner sending a few dollars, the person barely getting by who still helps feed the hungry | *Start giving to others* (2016) |
| **Start where you are** | You don't wait to be rich to begin giving. It's what you do with what you have | *Start giving to others*; *Be generous* (2021) |
| **An open hand to the poor** | Built on the instruction in Deuteronomy to open your hand wide to a poor neighbor, and on the call in Proverbs to stand up for the poor | *Be generous* |
| **Giving is a habit** | The only way to keep a generous attitude is to keep giving time, attention, money, and resources | *Be generous* |
| **Examine your "no"** | When you turn down a request for help, ask whether your reasons are good ones, not just valid ones | *Be generous* |
| **Plant trees, don't just hand out apples** | The most generous look past the immediate request toward lasting solutions that keep providing for generations | *Generosity* (2018) |
| **See solutions, and bring others in** | They see what could be if their time, energy, or money were added, and think of who else they could bring to the table | *Generosity* |
| **The right place matters more than recognition** | Where a gift goes matters more than any tax benefit or public credit | *Generosity* |

## 2. Where it matches this project

| Teaching | Already in our design |
|---|---|
| Lasting solutions over temporary ones | The riskiest assumption asks whether a new effort beats strengthening what exists (NEED §10); Principle 11 rejects dependency |
| Examine your "no" | The Judge and probe thresholds make every decision state its reasons |
| Recognition doesn't matter | Charter §4: recipients are never asked for public thanks, and never used for publicity without consent |

## 3. Where it differs — stated plainly

- **Reward.** The teaching frames giving as returned with blessing, drawing on
  Luke 6:38. That is a sincere theological view. `philanthropisch` asks for no
  return **from the people it serves** (Charter §4), and takes no position on a
  giver's own faith or motivation.
- **Faith as the frame.** The teaching is explicitly Christian. Help from
  `philanthropisch` carries no faith condition of any kind (Charter §4), so it
  is open to people of every faith and none.
- **Context.** The *Generosity* post is written about major donors who underwrite
  that ministry's own events. We are borrowing the qualities it describes, not its
  purpose.

## 4. The right person for the guide role

The words you were looking for. Drawn from the teaching above, plus what the
listening work itself requires.

**From the teaching:**
1. **Gives time and attention,** not just money, and has made a habit of it.
2. **Has an open hand toward people who are struggling,** and speaks up for them.
3. **Thinks in trees, not apples:** looks for lasting solutions rather than
   quick, visible ones.
4. **Sees solutions, and knows who else to bring in.**
5. **Cares where help goes more than who gets the credit.**

**From the work:**
6. **Listens before prescribing.** Comfortable hearing that the answer is
   something other than their own idea.
7. **Already trusted** by the community being listened to.
8. **Happy to send people elsewhere** when another organization serves them
   better.
9. **Attaches no conditions** to help, whatever their own beliefs.
10. **Keeps confidences,** and never shares anyone's hardship.

Many people fit this, and most of them are not famous. Your longtime pastor
(L1) and the outreach lead at a local church are both worth measuring against it.
The best guide is usually close by and already known.

## 5. Other traditions worth adding

This project should not rest on a single tradition. One worth adding next:
**Maimonides' eight levels of giving**, from Jewish tradition. Its highest level
is helping someone become self-sufficient, and it ranks giving where neither the
giver nor the recipient knows the other above giving where they do. Both ideas
map directly onto Principle 11 and Charter §4. Others can be added over time by
whoever inherits this work.
