## Why start with him as a guide

Starting as a guide rather than a site asks less of him, and gets more from him:

- **He can review the [listening guide](GUIDE.md)** before anyone else sees it,
  as the probe already requires of each host. He will know what sounds
  intrusive, what sounds naive, and what will not work.
- **He likely sees need firsthand.** Pastors are often among the first people
  told about a family running out of food. His view counts as one
  *provider-type* source, not as a finding.
- **He may open doors to the other two settings.** Pastors often know other
  pastors in denominational or regional networks. If his congregation is in
  one setting, he may know trusted people in a rural or small-town congregation,
  which is exactly how P2 and P3 get filled without cold outreach.

---

## Two safeguards if a congregation hosts

**Nobody should feel faith is a condition.** Sessions must be open to people of
any faith or none, contain no religious content, and be run so that no one feels
that taking part, or getting help, depends on belief or membership. A neutral
room, not the sanctuary, helps.

**The pastor's own role.** People may be less willing to admit hardship to their
own pastor, however much they trust him. It is a reason for care, not a reason
to exclude him. Options, for him to weigh in on:

- He recruits and hosts; someone else facilitates the sessions.
- He facilitates only sessions with people outside his own congregation.
- He is present for the opening and then leaves the room.

## First conversation with L1 — for the operator

An informal conversation, operator and pastor. Nothing to sign, nothing
promised. About 30–45 minutes.

**Say up front**
- What this is: listening before building anything, to find where people fall
  through the gaps in food help.
- What it is not: no program is being launched, and one possible result is
  simply backing organizations already doing the work, possibly including his.
- You want his advice first. Being a host is a separate question for later.

**Ask**
1. What does he see around food — who struggles, and when?
2. Where do people he knows go for help? What gets in their way?
3. Does the church run a pantry or food ministry, or work with one nearby?
4. Would he read the listening guide and tell you honestly what is wrong with it?
5. Would people talk openly in a session he hosted? Would they with him in the
   room?
6. Does he know trusted people — pastors or otherwise — in a rural community or a
   small town or suburb who might advise too?
7. Has anyone already asked his community about this recently?

**Do not**
- Ask him to share anything about specific people in his congregation.
- Promise funding, a program, or a stipend yet. The budget includes a host
  stipend, but offer it only once he is considering hosting.
- Ask him to recruit anyone before he has decided he wants to be involved.

**Afterwards:** tell me what he said, and I'll update L1 against the criteria,
revise the guide with his feedback, and follow up on any introductions he offers.
