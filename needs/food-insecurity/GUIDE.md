# Listening guide — food insecurity

> The playbook for [PROBE.md](PROBE.md), run identically in every pilot
> community. Three parts: a community session guide, a provider conversation
> guide, and a notes template that feeds the probe's tally.
> **Status: DRAFT.** Review with each community's host organization before first
> use; they will know what does not fit locally.

The single most important rule in this guide: **ask open questions first, and
record what people raise *before* anyone suggests a possible answer.** The
probe's metric counts *unprompted* gaps. One leading question can contaminate a
whole session.

---

## Part A — Community listening session

About 60 minutes. Small groups of 4–8, or one-to-one if preferred.

### Before the session

- [ ] Recruited through the host organization, never by cold outreach.
- [ ] Space chosen with the host: familiar, accessible, reachable without a car.
- [ ] Interpreter booked for any language participants speak.
- [ ] A meal or real food on the table, not just snacks.
- [ ] Compensation ready to **hand over at the start**, so it cannot be mistaken
      for payment for the "right" answers.
- [ ] A filled-in [referral sheet](REFERRALS.template.md) for every participant.
- [ ] One facilitator and one separate note-taker. Nobody records audio or video
      unless every person present has agreed out loud.
- [ ] Someone from the host organization available afterwards, in case a
      participant needs help today.

### Opening — say this, in your own words

> Thank you for coming. Here's your [compensation] before we start. It's yours
> whether you say a lot, a little, or decide to leave.
>
> We're here to listen. We're trying to understand what it's really like to get
> enough food around here, and what gets in the way.
>
> **We are not offering a program or a service today, and we can't promise one.**
> We may find the best thing we can do is support [host organization] and others
> already doing this work.
>
> You don't have to talk about yourself. Talking about "people around here" is
> completely fine. Skip any question. Leave any time.
>
> We won't write down your name or anything that identifies you. [Note-taker] is
> writing down what's said, not who said it. We'll delete the notes by [date].
>
> We'll come back and tell you what we heard from everyone, whatever it turns
> out to be.
>
> [Referral sheet] has places to get food and help, including today. It's yours
> to keep.
>
> Any questions before we begin?

### Questions — in this order

Speak in terms of "people around here". Never ask anyone to disclose their own
hardship. Let silence sit.

**1. Warm-up.** *"What's it like getting groceries around here?"*
Not about hunger yet. Listen for stores, distance, prices, transport.

**2. The hard stretch.** *"When money for food runs short — say near the end of
the month — what do people around here do?"*
Listen for the workarounds (NEED §3).

**3. Where people turn.** *"When people need help with food, where do they go?
What's that like?"*

**4. What gets in the way — the key question.** *"What makes it hard to get food
or help with food?"*
Then: *"What else?"* Ask it more than once.
**The note-taker marks everything said here as UNPROMPTED.**

**5. Tried and stopped.** *"Is there help people have tried and stopped using?
What happened?"*

**6. One change.** *"If one thing changed that would make the biggest difference,
what would it be?"*

**7. Who does it well.** *"Who's already doing a good job helping with this?"*
Listen for providers to support rather than duplicate (NEED §10).

**8. Prompted check — only now, after 4–7.** Read the list, one at a time, and ask
whether each comes up for people around here:

- Hours when help is open
- Getting there / transport
- Earning a bit too much to qualify for help
- Recent changes to SNAP (food stamps)
- Paperwork and applications
- Fresh food, or food that fits people's diets and cultures
- Having a way to store or cook food
- Feeling judged, or embarrassed to ask

**The note-taker marks everything said here as PROMPTED.** Prompted answers
explain the result; they never count toward the probe's primary metric.

**9. Close.** *"Is there anything important we didn't ask about?"*
Then repeat: what happens next, when you'll report back, the referral sheet,
thank you.

### Never

- **Never ask** anyone's name, income, benefits status, or immigration or
  citizenship status. Recent SNAP rule changes affect some noncitizens, and a
  question about status can frighten people away from help they are entitled to.
- **Never advise** anyone on whether they qualify for a program. Point to the
  referral sheet or the host organization.
- **Never promise** a service, a follow-up program, or a timeline for one.
- **Never push** past discomfort. If someone becomes distressed, pause, offer a
  break, and bring in the host.
- **If someone needs food today,** stop being a researcher: connect them with the
  host staff and the referral sheet straight away.

## Part B — Provider conversation

About 45 minutes, with a food bank, pantry, school family liaison, WIC clinic,
211 office, faith group, or mutual-aid group.

### Opening

> We're trying to understand where people fall through the gaps, before we do
> anything at all, and one real possibility is that the right move is to support
> organizations like yours. We won't ask for anything that identifies a client.
> We'll share what we learn with you.

### Questions — in this order

**1. Who you serve.** *"Who comes to you, and how has that changed over the past
year?"*

**2. Who you can't serve — the key question.** *"Who do you have to turn away, or
can't reach? Why?"*
**Mark UNPROMPTED.**

**3. What your records show.** *"Do you track visits, turn-aways, waitlists, or
requests you can't meet? Could you share totals or trends — nothing about any
individual?"*
This is where stated evidence becomes revealed evidence. A provider agreeing to
share counts toward the probe's secondary metric.

**4. Recent changes — prompted, only after 2 and 3.** *"Have you noticed changes
since the new SNAP rules took effect?"* **Mark PROMPTED.**

**5. What would help most.** *"If you had more of one thing — money, volunteers,
food, space, transport, staff time, data — what would it be?"*

**6. What a newcomer gets wrong.** *"What do new organizations get wrong when they
try to help with food here?"*

**7. Connections.** *"Who else should we talk to? Would you be willing to host a
listening session with people you serve?"*
Agreeing to host counts toward the secondary metric.

### Never

- Never ask for client names, contact details, or records about individuals.
- Never ask a provider to recruit clients until the provider itself has agreed
  (PROBE §3).
- Never imply funding is coming. It may, but not yet.

### How the tally works

1. For each community, collect all rows marked **UNPROMPTED** and **specific**.
2. Group rows describing the same gap. Wording will differ, so grouping is a
   judgment: record every grouping decision and its reason, so it can be checked.
3. Count independent sources per gap: each provider organization counts once;
   each community participant counts once.
4. Mark whether provider records corroborate the gap.
5. Apply PROBE §5 per community, then compare across communities.

**Guard against the note-taker's own bias:** a second person regroups the rows
from a sample of sessions without seeing the first grouping. If the two groupings
disagree often, the categories are too vague and must be fixed before the tally
counts.
