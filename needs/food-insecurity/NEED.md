# Need Brief — Local food insecurity

> **Status: DRAFT.** Sensing, stage 2. Not yet judged.
> Venture: `philanthropisch`. Based on the RZN AI Need Brief template.

This brief is written almost entirely in *unknowns*, and that is correct. The
operator named a cause; nobody has yet asked the people living it. Per
Principle 12, this brief's first job is to say plainly what we do not know, and
how we will find out without deciding on anyone's behalf.

**Scope: not bound to one location.** The need is general. Its *shape* is local,
though: which gap matters differs between, say, a rural county with no grocery
store and a city where pantry hours clash with shift work. So evidence is
gathered in several deliberately different pilot communities (see
[PROBE.md](PROBE.md) §2), and the comparison between them is itself evidence. A
gap that converges across communities points toward an offer that can scale; a
gap found in only one points toward backing that community's own providers.

---

## 1. The need, in one sentence

> A household, in any community, that runs short of food, or of money for food,
> and cannot get what it needs from the help that already exists, because of
> **[unknown: the specific gap this brief exists to find]**.

**The test:** the sentence does not yet pass. It names a situation but not the
gap. Filling that blank is the purpose of the probe.

## 6. Why now

**Nationally, two things changed** (detail in [DATA-national §4](DATA-national.md)):

- **SNAP rules narrowed under H.R. 1.** Work requirements now reach adults up to
  64 and parents of children 14 and over; exemptions for veterans, people
  experiencing homelessness, and former foster youth were removed; some
  noncitizens lost eligibility from 2025-11-01; states' share of administrative
  costs rises to 75% from 2026-10-01. Rising pantry demand is *predicted*, not
  yet shown.
- **The official national survey ended.** USDA's 2024 report is the last, so
  local provider records may soon be the best evidence available anywhere.

**Locally: unknown.** Per pilot community, check food prices, grocery store or
pantry closures, and changes in the food bank's supply.

## 7. The shape of an offer

**Deliberately empty.** Designing an offer before listening is exactly what
Principle 12 forbids.

Whatever offer eventually emerges must still fill all of these:

| | |
|---|---|
| What the person would receive | not yet designed |
| How they could decline it | must exist |
| How they could undo it | must exist |
| How they would know why they received it | must exist |

One legitimate outcome is that the best offer is not a new service at all, but
funding, volunteers, or logistics for providers who are already closing the gap.

## 8. The manufactured-want check

- **Does the offer create the dependency it then sells?** No offer exists yet.
  The specific risk here is a service that people come to rely on and that ends
  when funding does, leaving them worse off than before it existed.
- **Does its success depend on people using it more than serves them?** No offer
  yet. Success must be measured by need met, never by visits.
- **Would this need exist if we never built anything?** Yes. Food insecurity is
  well documented in general. Its specific local shape is not yet known.
- **Who could be harmed if this works exactly as intended?**
  - **Existing providers**, if a new actor duplicates their work or competes for
    the same donors and volunteers.
  - **Participants**, if listening raises expectations of help that never
    arrives, or if taking part carries stigma.
  - **Anyone whose information is collected**, if it is kept or shared carelessly.

## 10. Riskiest assumption

> There is a specific, local gap that existing providers are not already closing,
> and `philanthropisch` would close it better by acting directly than by funding
> or supporting those providers.

Because the scope spans communities, the assumption has a second half: that at
least one such gap is *shared* across different communities, rather than every
community's gap being its own.

The need itself is not the risky part. The risky part is whether a *new* actor is
the right answer. A finding that the best move is to back the food bank is a real
and useful result, not a failure.
