## 2. The probe

| | |
|---|---|
| Pilot communities | **Profile adopted; specific places and hosts not yet named.** See *Choosing the pilots* below; leads tracked in [PILOTS.md](PILOTS.md). P1: [FILL] · P2: [FILL] · P3: [FILL] |
| Type | The same listening playbook run in every pilot community: sessions with community members, and structured conversations with providers |
| What a provider experiences | A ~45-minute conversation: what they cannot meet, who they turn away and why, what their own data shows, and what would help them most |
| What a community member experiences | An invitation from a provider they already know; a ~60-minute small-group or one-to-one conversation; paid for their time; food provided; anonymous; told at the start that nothing is being offered yet and what happens with what they say |
| Where they encounter it | At provider sites or a neutral community space participants choose |
| Cost ceiling | **$12,500 cash** across up to 3 communities (about $4,200 each; $8,400 if only 2), **including** any extension; and **40 person-hours** per community. Reaching either ceiling halts the probe; it is never overrun. Estimate and assumptions below |
| Duration | **6 weeks** from launch, fixed. The end date is set on the launch gate (§6); the one permitted extension (§5) adds at most 3 weeks |

### Choosing the pilots

**Profile.** Three communities that differ in the ways most likely to change what
the gap is. Rates are national 2024 figures from [DATA-national](DATA-national.md).

| Pilot | Setting | Why this one | Gaps to stay alert to (never to suggest) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Urban neighborhood | Urban households are food insecure at 16.0% | Help's opening hours versus shift work; cost of living |
| **P2** | Rural, preferably in the South | Rural rate 15.9%; more than 8 in 10 of the highest-rate counties are rural or in the South (modelled) | Distance, transport, access to a grocery store |
| **P3** | Suburban or small town | Lower average rate (11.9%), but often fewer services and less visible need | Whether a gap exists even where need is less visible |

A gap that shows up in settings this different is strong evidence of a shared
gap. One that shows up in only one points to backing that community's providers.

**Every pilot must meet all of these:**

1. **A host organization that has agreed**, such as a pantry, the regional food
   bank, a school, a faith group, or a mutual-aid group. Not one that merely
   exists.
2. **A listener that people there already trust**: ideally someone local, or
   someone the host vouches for.
3. **A food bank or pantry that serves the area** and will share aggregate
   records, since without provider data nothing can PROMOTE (§5).
4. **Reachable within the budget**, including the $300 travel line.
5. **Known language needs**, so interpretation can be booked.
6. **No recent similar listening effort there**, per the host. Asking people the
   same questions again without anything changing is its own harm.

**Relationships outrank statistics.** A place where the operator or someone
trusted already has ties beats a statistically ideal place where nobody knows
anyone. The profile is a preference; the six criteria are requirements.

**The probe runs with two pilots if only two qualify.** It does not add a pilot
that fails a criterion just to reach three.

### Cost estimate, per community

Planned for **4 sessions and 24 participants**, double the 12-person floor in §5,
because some people who say yes will not come.

| Item | Assumption | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Participant compensation | 24 people × $50 for a ~60-minute session, handed over at the start | $1,200 |
| Food | 4 sessions × 8 people (6 participants, 2 staff) × $15 | $480 |
| Space | 4 sessions × $100, if the host cannot provide it free | $400 |
| Interpretation | 2 of 4 sessions × $300 | $600 |
| Host organization stipend | For staff time spent recruiting and hosting | $500 |
| Referral sheets and supplies | Printing, translation of the sheet, notebooks | $100 |
| Listener travel | Varies widely by distance | $300 |
| **Subtotal** | | **$3,580** |
| Contingency | 15% | $540 |
| **Per community** | | **≈ $4,120** |

**Hours, per community:** 4 sessions × 2 hours (setup plus session) × 2 people
= 16; 3 provider conversations × 1.5 hours = 4.5; notes, tally, and the
second-person regrouping check ≈ 10; recruitment coordination ≈ 6. About **37
person-hours**, capped at 40.

**These are assumptions, not researched rates.** Compensation, interpretation,
and space costs vary a lot by region; the host organization in each community
should sanity-check them. Changing an assumption before launch is fine. After
launch, the ceiling is fixed.

**Note on the operator's time:** 40 hours per community does not fit inside the
two cockpit hours. This is exactly the kind of human work the principles keep
out of the autopilot. Either it is budgeted as real time, or someone on the
ground does the listening.

**Why listening first:** this is the cheapest probe that builds nothing, and the
only one Principle 12 allows before an offer exists.

## 3. Honesty rules

For the operator to tick before launch. The last six are specific to this probe.

- [ ] Everyone is told what this is: listening, with no service being offered yet.
- [ ] No fake scarcity, fake deadlines, or fake social proof.
- [ ] Declining to take part is as easy as accepting, and costs nothing.
- [ ] Only de-identified notes are kept; no recording without explicit consent;
      notes deleted on [FILL: date] unless participants opt in to more.
- [ ] Participants are told what happens next, including if we decide not to
      build anything.
- [ ] No money is taken from participants.
- [ ] The community is asked, not merely measured (Principle 12). How:
      recruitment through trusted providers, not cold outreach.
- [ ] **No promises.** Nobody is told a service is coming.
- [ ] **A referral sheet at every session**: 211 and local pantries with hours and
      addresses, so no one leaves a conversation about hunger without a way to
      get food today.
- [ ] **Compensation does not depend on what anyone says.**
- [ ] **Providers agree before their clients are approached**, and are not asked
      to hand over anything that identifies a client.
- [ ] **Language access**: sessions offered in the languages the community speaks.
- [ ] **Findings are reported back** to participants and providers, in plain
      language, whatever they turn out to be.

## 4. The metric

A listening probe produces mostly *stated* evidence. What makes it trustworthy
is requiring that what people say is corroborated by what providers' records
show, which is *revealed* evidence.

| Metric | Exact definition | How it is counted | Why it reveals demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: convergent gap** | One *specific* gap — who is not getting what, and when — named *unprompted* by independent sources, and corroborated by provider data | Tally of de-identified notes. A "source" is one provider organization or one community participant | Independent convergence plus records is far harder to produce by accident, or by our own leading questions, than any single account |
| Secondary: provider commitment | A provider agrees to share operational data or to co-host sessions | Count | Costly behavior: it spends their time and trust |

**Specific** means describable as "working parents cannot reach pantry hours",
not "people need more food". **Unprompted** means named before any list of
possible gaps is shown.

## 5. Pre-registered thresholds

**Adopted by the operator on 2026-09-12, before any data was collected.**

| Outcome | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Too little data** | fewer than **3** providers, or fewer than **12** community participants across at least **2** separate sessions, reached in a community → the probe failed to run there; not a verdict on the need |
| **PROMOTE** (to an *offer* probe, not to a Charter) | one gap named unprompted by ≥ **2** providers **and** by ≥ **40%** of that community's participants, **and** corroborated by provider data |
| **KILL** (this hypothesis, not the cause) | no gap reaches those bars |
| **EXTEND** | a gap reaches one bar but not the other: permitted once, **3** more weeks, same thresholds |

**Group sessions and the 40% bar.** In a group, once one person names a gap
others often agree. Agreement still counts, since it is unprompted by the
facilitator, but it is not fully independent. So the 40% must be reached
**across at least 2 separate sessions**: a gap raised in only one session cannot
PROMOTE, however many people in that room agreed.

**Why these numbers.**
- **3 providers, 12 participants:** the smallest reach at which convergence means
  something. Below it, two people agreeing could be chance.
- **2 providers:** one provider's view can reflect that one organization;
  two independent organizations naming the same gap is a pattern.
- **40% of participants:** high enough that a gap must be broadly felt rather than
  one group's concern, low enough that a real but not universal gap can still
  clear it, given that people are never shown a list before naming gaps.
- **Provider-data corroboration:** the only part of the rule that is revealed
  rather than stated evidence. Without it, nothing PROMOTEs.
- **One 3-week extension:** enough for a few more sessions; allowed only once, so
  a probe cannot be extended until it happens to pass.

The rules above are applied **in each pilot community separately**. The results
are then compared:

| Across communities | Finding |
|---|---|
| The same gap PROMOTEs in ≥ **2** communities | **Shared gap.** Candidate for an offer designed to work in many places |
| A gap PROMOTEs in one community only | **Local gap.** Candidate for backing that community's providers, not for a scalable offer |
| Different gaps PROMOTE in different communities | Record each as local; no shared offer yet |
| Nothing PROMOTEs anywhere | KILL, as below |

A playbook that cannot be run the same way in a second community was not a
playbook. Differences in how it had to be run are recorded in §9.

**An important kind of KILL:** if the converging gaps are ones providers say they
could close with more resources, the finding is *support existing providers*.
That is recorded as the result in §8, and becomes its own Need Brief. It is a
direction, not a dead end.
